Request-length presets

Where this scenario’s demand comes from

Requests ≈ tokens ÷ (mean input + mean output)
Inference FLOPs ≈ 2 × (N input D input + N output D output )

The token split is inferred from your assumed input/output lengths. With the same active parameter count for both phases, this reduces to 2ND. FLOPs are a rough, uncached parameter-matmul proxy, not GPU hours or actual cost. Attention, routing, speculative decoding, cache reuse, precision, batching, and input/output utilization can change serving work and cost substantially. For MoE, active and total parameters serve different roles.

Why “1 billion requests” is not a fixed amount of compute

Sardana et al. study training choices under a model’s total future inference demand. Their simple accounting uses training FLOPs ≈ 6ND and inference FLOPs ≈ 2ND inf , where inference tokens include input and output. The request workload in Figure 6 has 70 input and 215 output tokens: 1 billion such requests means 285 billion tokens . A scenario with 64,000 input and 4,000 output tokens needs 68 trillion tokens for the same number of requests.

The public model-ranking dataset provides combined tokens, not model-level request counts or an input/output split. Changing the request-length preset changes request equivalents; it does not change the already observed token volume. The paper’s hardware utilization and monetary cost assumptions are separate from this token-based projection.

06 / WHAT THIS STUDY CAN AND CANNOT TELL US

Definitions, reproducibility, and related work

Popularity is not retirement

Ranks measure token traffic among public OpenRouter text models. A sustained top-50 exit is our observable event. Model availability, quality, capability leadership, user retention, and use outside OpenRouter are different outcomes.

Censoring is part of the result

Models present at the archive’s beginning have unknown earlier history. Models still active at the cutoff have unknown future lifetime. Cohort survival estimates account for right censoring and exclude boundary entries; completed-only means would favor short-lived models.

Exact identities matter

Dated checkpoints and serving variants remain separate. “Open-weight” means publicly downloadable weights under some license, not necessarily OSI open source. The reviewed status is not a claim that every checkpoint was open throughout its earlier traffic history.

Demand has two unknowns

Unreported individual tokens can be bounded within covered dates using the rank-50 cutoff and aggregate tail, but pre-archive and missing-date demand remain unknown. Future demand and average tokens per request are assumptions. No account analytics or private prompts are used.

Similar efforts—and what they contribute

  • OpenRouter: Model Half-Life compares lab-user retention and model launches. It motivates cohort comparisons, but its retention measure is not top-50 residence.
  • Vercel AI Gateway Production Index, November 2026 follows production adoption and migration. It is an external comparison with a different customer population, not extra observations for this survival fit.
  • Birth, Life, and Death of AI Models distinguishes technical from usage obsolescence. A threshold-based useful life depends on how obsolescence is defined.
  • The Emerging Market for Intelligence studies the market for model APIs. Workload differences and routing matter when interpreting aggregate demand.
  • IAPS OpenRouter Open-Source Rankings provides a related open-weight usage dashboard. This study retains exact dated IDs and explicit censored demand to avoid merging successive releases into one lifetime.
  • Yukio Takagi: model usage lifespan analysis tracks post-peak usage retention. We show absolute tokens beside platform share because the two can move in opposite directions.
