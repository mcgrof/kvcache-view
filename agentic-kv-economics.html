What does one idle context cost?

A context waits while an agent runs a tool or a person thinks. Compare the cost of that context’s storage space with computing it again once. Then check the separate questions: how fast can it return, will it be reused, and how much capacity does the fleet need?

    Decimal units throughout: 1 GB = 10⁹ bytes. 80,000 × 890 B is exactly 71.2 MB; “72 MB” is a rounded example. Five years uses 365 days/year. GPU-seconds × price assumes exclusive use of all eight GPUs; continuous batching needs measured marginal GPU time or billed cost per completed request.

    Storage, transfer, and compute assumptions

    Wear is off by default: adding full endurance amortization to full time depreciation can count drive capital twice. Its separate sensitivity is price × bytes written ÷ (DWPD × lifetime days). It excludes write-path GPU time, serialization, network fees, replication, and redundancy; enter measured overheads where available. Drive rates, prices, MFU, and fixed/replay time are editable scenarios, not matched benchmarks. Effective transfer bandwidth is capped by the shared path. Queueing and competing requests can lower it further.

    What the research changes

    Evidence Implication for this analysis Boundary
    KV Residency, October 2026 In a Deep Research replay, a one-hour idle cap reduces peak retention demand 3.6× while recomputing calls rise from 4.07% to 4.53%. Placement adds another loss of reuse. 121M+ production calls; memory uses a public Llama-2-70B proxy. Quantized-retention simulations omit quality and transfer costs.
    DualPath, 2026 Storage-facing links can bottleneck cross-turn reuse. Model the whole storage-to-GPU route and concurrent demand. Four drives’ summed sequential speeds do not establish end-to-end restore throughput.
    ECHO, OSDI 2026 Sparse attention permits a DRAM-backed MLA cache and a smaller GPU working set. Indexer keys remain GPU-resident. This is active decode offloading on 8×H20, not an NVMe resume benchmark.
    CacheScout, 2026 Agent-transition prediction can improve eviction decisions and warm likely next contexts. Its proactive warmup is recomputation, not proof of SSD prefetch speed.
    AgentKV, 2026 Think, act, and tool phases attend differently; phase-aware eviction can preserve more useful state. Lossy retention requires task-quality validation. Benchmark gains are not applied as multipliers here.
    DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, 2026 890 B/token global KV and bounded SWA replay sharply reduce persistent state. Bounded replay is approximate and takes compute. A smaller persistent image is not the entire live HBM working set.

    Equations, provenance, and corrections to the draft 
    context bytes = tokens × persisted bytes/token + fixed checkpoint bytes
capacity cost/day = bytes/1e9 × $/GB ÷ (365 × service years)
days equal to one recompute = recompute cost ÷ capacity cost/day
restore seconds = bytes / effective bytes/second + fixed overhead + replay
expected net = P(valid credited return) × (recompute cost − restore cost)
               − optional write allocation − daily holding cost × E[held days]
average parked bytes = arrivals/day × E[held days] × bytes/context

    No return means holding through expiration. The cache-cohort calculation scales compute credit with uncached-token fractions, which is only a rough proxy when attention is nonlinear. Exact-prefix identity must include model/checkpoint, adapters, tokenizer, positions, and cache representation; compaction or model changes can invalidate a stored image. Sharing, deduplication, replication, write-path time, and peak occupancy need separate measurements.

    The original draft mixed cohorts, assumed a universal five-minute miss, double-discounted switches, treated a truncated histogram as a complete lifetime distribution, assumed infinite free bandwidth, and called long storage allowances “indefinite.” These are corrected here. The unverified 2.9 s H100 calibration is not used as a measured universal MFU. FLOP estimates are sensitivity tools and omit communication, non-matmul kernels, scheduling, padding, and precision-specific bottlenecks.
